# Structural Options Generator

**Stage:** 5 · The Branch
**Source:** Dan Packard §2.1(a) — primary
**Version:** v0.1 SCAFFOLD — option framework functional; CN's structuring playbook pending extraction.

## What it does

Turns "the client is a Seller (or Buyer) and here's their goal" into the 3–4 structural paths Dan would sketch on the whiteboard — each with pros, cons, tax considerations, and what has to be true for it to work — so the partner walks into the conversation with a map instead of a blank page.

## Inputs

- Classifier output (posture + prepared/unprepared read)
- Owner goals: liquidity need, timeline, legacy/employee concerns, family involvement
- Normalized financials and rough valuation range if developed
- Entity structure and state footprint

## Process

1. Select candidate structures for the posture and goal `[EXTRACT: Dan's structure menu — which options he actually puts on the board for each situation type, e.g., third-party sale vs. ESOP vs. management buyout vs. family transfer for a prepared seller; platform vs. tuck-in vs. organic for a buyer]`.
2. For each candidate, build the standard read: mechanics in three sentences · pros · cons · tax considerations flagged for §2.1(c) review · financing reality · timeline · what kills it `[EXTRACT: Dan's kill-criteria per structure]`.
3. Rank by fit to the stated goal — and say explicitly which goal each structure sacrifices, because every structure sacrifices something.
4. Route tax items to Tax-Strategy Surfacer; valuation-dependent items to Valuation & Payout Modeler.

## Output

A 3–4 option comparison memo, one page per option plus a comparison table, in whiteboard-honest language. Ends with "what we'd need to confirm" per option.

## Guardrails

- Tax considerations are **surfaced, never concluded** — every tax line carries "subject to §2.1(c) partner review."
- Never present one option as the answer; the deliverable is the map. The partner and client choose.
- No yellow-brick-road: options reflect this client's facts, not a standard sequence (Dan's explicit design constraint from the audit).

## Extraction session agenda — Dan (60–90 min, whiteboard session, record it)

1. Take two past engagements — one seller, one buyer — and rebuild the options he actually presented. Capture the menu and what was deliberately left off.
2. Per structure: the three questions he asks to qualify it in or out.
3. The failure stories: structures that closed and shouldn't have, or died late — and the early warning missed.
4. The handoff line: where structuring judgment ends and the tax team's work begins.
