# Meeting Re-Context

**Stage:** 6 · The Conversation
**Source:** Daniel Hopkins §2.1(d) — primary
**Version:** v0.5 DEEP DRAFT — process skill; near-final. Extraction calibrates emphasis, doesn't create structure.

## What it does

Reads the war room and produces the pre-meeting context sheet: where the relationship stands, what was promised, what's open, and what this meeting needs to accomplish — the institutional-memory answer to the audit's finding that context lives in the partner's head and makes Dan a single point of failure.

## Inputs

- The war room: Client Profile, meeting history, deliverables sent, open loops
- The upcoming meeting: purpose if stated, attendees if known
- Time since last touch (drives depth — a week needs a nudge; a year needs the full rebuild)

## Process

1. **The story so far** — five lines maximum: who the client is, the goal in their words, current posture, where the engagement stands.
2. **Last conversation** — decisions made, commitments given (both directions), the emotional register if noted (was the client anxious? energized? stalling?).
3. **Open loops** — from the Open-Loop Tracker: what we owe them (with status — arriving prepared with an overdue item unaddressed is worse than not meeting), what they owe us, what's unresolved.
4. **What's changed since** — new documents, analyses completed, regulatory flags, life events on the profile.
5. **This meeting** — its stated purpose, the three things it should accomplish, and one thing NOT to raise (from watch items).
6. Age-check everything: facts older than their shelf life get a "confirm this is still true" mark rather than being asserted.

## Output

A one-page context sheet, readable in five minutes, ordered exactly as above. If an Advisory Brief exists for this meeting, the context sheet fronts it; if not, it stands alone.

## Guardrails

- One page. The skill's discipline is compression — a ten-page history defeats the purpose.
- Commitments are quoted with dates, not paraphrased.
- Gaps are stated ("no record of the March conversation — check with Rob") — pretending completeness is how partners get blindsided.
- Watch items (family dynamics, sensitivities) appear on the internal sheet only, flagged as such.

## Extraction session agenda — Hopkins (20 min, calibration)

1. What he actually rereads before a meeting today, in what order — does the sheet match his eye?
2. A live test: generate the sheet for one of his real upcoming meetings; mark what's missing and what's noise.
3. The shelf-life rule: how old can a "fact" be before it needs re-confirming, by fact type?
