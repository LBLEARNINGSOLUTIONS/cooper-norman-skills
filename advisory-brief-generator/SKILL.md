# Advisory Brief Generator

**Stage:** 6 · The Conversation
**Source:** Daniel Hopkins §2.1(d) — primary
**Version:** v0.1 SCAFFOLD — brief structure functional; CN's internal format and the 3-talking-points discipline pending extraction.

## What it does

Assembles everything the war room knows into the internal brief format partners actually use: the analysis distilled, the three talking points, and the forward-looking frame — built for the meeting inversion the audit captured: **5 minutes on the past, 25 on the future.**

## Inputs

- Completed analyses: Smell-Test, Benchmarks, Classification, Options, Valuation — whatever exists
- Client Profile: the goal in the owner's words, watch items, relationship history
- Meeting purpose: first advisory conversation vs. options presentation vs. War Room prep
- CN brief format `[COLLECT/EXTRACT: the internal brief and "3 talking points" artifacts referenced in the workflow map — get real examples from Hopkins]`

## Process

1. Distill each analysis to its decision-relevant core — what the partner needs to hold in their head, not everything the analysis found.
2. Build the **three talking points** `[EXTRACT: how Hopkins picks them — the selection discipline is the methodology]` — each with its supporting number and the question it opens for the client.
3. Frame forward: per talking point, the future-tense version ("here's what this makes possible") — the 25-minutes-on-the-future engine.
4. Pre-empt: the questions the client will ask, with the honest answers — including the ones the partner fears (the audit's finding: *partners avoid questions they fear they can't answer* — the brief exists to remove that fear).
5. War Room escalation check: complexity signals that say this needs the whiteboard and a second partner before the client sees it `[EXTRACT: the escalation criteria Scott and Dan use]`.

## Output

A 2-page internal brief: situation in five lines · three talking points with support · forward frames · anticipated questions with answers · open loops relevant to this meeting · escalate-or-proceed call. Never client-facing (that's Branded Report Generator's job).

## Guardrails

- Three talking points means three. The discipline is the value.
- Every number in the brief traces to an analysis in the war room — a brief is not a place for new math.
- The feared-questions section is mandatory, not optional — it's the confidence mechanism this skill exists for.

## Extraction session agenda — Hopkins (45 min)

1. Collect three real internal briefs (sanitized) — structure, length, voice.
2. The talking-point selection: same analysis, which three points and why; what got cut.
3. The feared questions: what partners actually avoid, by engagement type, and how he answers each.
4. War Room escalation: what tips a call from simple to whiteboard.
